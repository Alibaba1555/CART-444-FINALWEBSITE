Game · 2024

PACMAN Variation Jam

This project begins with a simple question: what happens when a familiar system starts to break?

Based on the classic structure of Pac-Man, I developed a series of variations that gradually distort movement, perception, and spatial logic. Each mode is not just a gameplay change, but a small experiment on how rules shape experience.

Instead of designing a completely new game, I chose to work within an existing system — and push it until it feels unfamiliar.

View on GitHub ↗
Process

The project was developed through iterative experiments. I started from the original Pac-Man mechanics, then introduced controlled disruptions — limiting vision, cloning agents, deforming the maze, and altering pacing.

Each iteration tested a specific question: how much can the system change before it stops being recognizable?

Reflection

This project revealed how strongly players rely on learned patterns. Even small changes — like reduced visibility or slight spatial distortion — can create tension and uncertainty.

Looking back, the project could be pushed further by introducing more dynamic systems, such as adaptive AI or procedural level changes, allowing the game to respond to the player rather than remain fixed.

    Photography

    I started photography as a hobby in early 2026, partly inspired by the film The Secret Life of Walter Mitty. I enjoy bringing my camera on walks and trips, photographing places, people, and everyday things that catch my attention.

    Photography has helped me notice small details I used to walk past, like light on a building or something happening on a quiet street. It has also made me more comfortable starting conversations with people I do not know. Sometimes taking a photo gives me a chance to ask a question, hear a story, or see a place from someone else's perspective.

    I am still learning, and I enjoy having a reason to go outside, meet people, and find something interesting or beautiful in everyday life. These three collections show some of my photographs, with more on Instagram.

    About Me

    I'm Tianshun, a BFA student in Computation Arts at Concordia University in Montréal. I work with creative programming, interactive media, and games, with a background in filmmaking and graphic design. My background in digital media at Collège Jean-de-Brébeuf introduced me to visual storytelling; at Concordia, I've been exploring how code and interaction can become part of that process.

    My Practice

    I'm interested in how people experience and respond to what I make. My projects range from browser games that change familiar rules to experiments that translate environmental data into sound. I enjoy building something I can try, adjusting it, and seeing how a small change in timing, layout, or feedback affects the experience.

    I have a growing interest in designing around people's needs. Outside my project work, photography is a personal hobby I picked up in early 2026.

    Experience & Background

    In 2026, I completed an AI and Innovation internship at BMW Technology Office China in Shanghai. I explored AI-assisted workflows, supported technology research, and created visual and multimedia content. I also co-organized and facilitated an internal AI workshop with activities in prompt design, image generation, and interactive prototyping. Working across these tasks gave me experience communicating technical ideas and collaborating with people from different backgrounds.

    Selected Tools

    JavaScript, HTML/CSS, p5.js, Phaser, Max/MSP, Unreal Engine, Adobe Creative Suite, Generative AI, Prompt Design, and AI-assisted Prototyping.
